Maufant Youth Project is registered with the Jersey Charity Commissioner as charity number 340, under the Charities (Jersey) Law 2014. The certificate is signed by the Commissioner and our entry is held on the public Register of Charities.

Our registered charitable purpose is the advancement of citizenship or community development, and the three objects set out above are the ones the Commissioner holds on record for us.

Our Mission

Three Objects, One Purpose

These are the charitable objects as registered with the Jersey Charity Commissioner. Everything we do has to trace back to one of them.

01

Support youth work opportunities

To support the development of youth work opportunities for young people, so that age, income or postcode never decides who gets to join in.

02

Promote personal and social development

To promote young people's personal and social development — confidence, teamwork, new skills and the resilience to navigate the years ahead.

03

Enable a voice that's heard

To enable young people to have a voice which is heard in their local parish and in the Island of Jersey — not tokenistically, but in the rooms where decisions are made.

"The income and property of the charity are applied solely towards the promotion of the objects."

How We Work

The Principles That Guide Us

Free to Attend

A young person's ability to pay should never be the reason they miss out. Sessions are free, and we quietly cover costs where it matters.

Local & Accountable

We are Jersey-registered, volunteer-run and answerable to our members, their families and our parish. Our accounts are filed as required.

Led by Young People

Members help shape the programme, plan the trips and speak up about what the Project should be doing next. It's their club.

Governance in Plain English

How the Charity Actually Runs

Small charity, small overheads — but the same obligations as any other. Here is what sits behind a Tuesday night at the Centre.

Trustees, meetings and paperwork

The Project is governed by a committee of unpaid trustees who meet through the year to agree the programme, review the accounts and keep an eye on safeguarding. The trustees are also the people who run the sessions — there is no office and no salaried staff between the money and the young people.

As a registered Jersey charity we file our accounts with the Charity Commissioner, hold an annual general meeting open to members and families, and keep written policies covering safeguarding, health and safety, data protection and volunteer recruitment.

Safeguarding

A named safeguarding lead, a written policy, references and background checks for every session volunteer, and two adults present at all times. If you have a concern about a young person — at the Centre or anywhere else — you can reach us on safeguarding@maufantyouth.org.

Money

We hold no core public funding and no paid staff. Income comes from donations, local businesses, grants and fundraising, and it goes out again as snacks, equipment, coach hire and free holiday places. Reserves are kept to roughly one term of running costs, not more.

Working with others

We work alongside families, local schools, the parish and Island-wide youth services, and we are a member of the Association of Jersey Charities. Where another organisation is better placed to help a young person, we will say so and make the introduction.

Our Story So Far

A Jersey Project, Year by Year

The Beginning

A club with nowhere to go

Local families and volunteers start running informal youth sessions in the east of the Island, using whatever space they can borrow.

The Centre Opens

A permanent home at Maufant

The Maufant Variety Youth Centre becomes the Project's home on La Grande Route de St Martin — a purpose-built space shared with the wider community.

Registration

Registered Charity No. 340

The Project was registered with the Jersey Charity Commissioner on 16 August 2020, and is a member of the Association of Jersey Charities as AJC505.

Growing

Juniors, Seniors and the holidays

The weekly timetable expands to separate sessions for younger and older members, and a holiday programme keeps the doors open all year.

Today

Still volunteer-run, still local

Every session is staffed by unpaid volunteers, funded by donations from Islanders and local businesses. There is no head office and no core public funding.
