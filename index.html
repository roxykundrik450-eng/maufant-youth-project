Our Charitable Purpose

Registered to Advance Community

Maufant Youth Project is registered with the Jersey Charity Commissioner for the advancement of citizenship or community development.

01

Youth Work Opportunities

To support the development of youth work opportunities for young people living in Jersey — whatever their background.

02

Personal & Social Growth

To promote their personal and social development, building confidence, resilience and friendships that last beyond the club.

03

A Voice That's Heard

To enable young people to have a voice which is heard — in their local parish and across the Island of Jersey.

The income and property of the charity are applied solely towards the promotion of these objects.

What Happens at the Centre

Six Things You Can Walk In and Do

The Centre is a sports hall, a kitchen, a workshop and a living room in one building. Nothing has to be booked, auditioned for or already be good at — you turn up, join in when you want, and sit it out when you don't.

Every week

Sport & Games

Table tennis, pool, badminton and whatever tournament the group decides on next. Members set the hall up and run the leagues themselves.

Seniors

Kitchen & Life Skills

Cooking a meal for the group, planning a food shop on a budget, bike maintenance, basic first aid. Practical things that are actually useful.

Juniors & Seniors

Making & Creating

Clay, paint, printing, sewing and building. The messy sessions are the ones that fill up fastest, and the work ends up on the Centre walls.

Wednesdays

Music & Media

Guitars, a drum kit, a PA and a laptop. Start a band, record something, run the Centre's socials or make a short film about the Project.

Holidays

Trips & Outdoors

Beach days, coastal walks, kayaking, climbing and the occasional residential. The Island is the playground and the holiday programme uses it.

Drop in

Games Room & Space

Board games, chess, consoles and a sofa that has seen better days. Not every young person wants a structured activity, and that is fine.

And the part that is not a game

Young People in the Room Where It’s Decided

Alongside the club nights, a project group meets to work on the things members actually raise — the bus that stops running too early, what there is to do in the holidays, how the Centre should spend its money. Their conclusions get written up and taken to parish and Island level by the young people themselves, not by us on their behalf.

It is the slowest work we do and the reason the charity exists: our third charitable object is to enable young people to have a voice which is heard.

How We Work

Youth Work, Not Babysitting

Anyone can put a table tennis table in a hall. What makes it youth work is the method behind it — and it is the same method whether a young person turns up for the sport or for the company.

Same adults, every week

Trust is built by turning up consistently. Members know who will be behind the counter on a Tuesday, and that is worth more than any programme.

Planned with members

The term programme is agreed with the young people who use it. If they want a tournament instead of a workshop, we run the tournament.

Safeguarding first

Volunteers are recruited, referenced and trained before they lead a session. Two adults are always present, and there is a named safeguarding contact.

Free, and open to all

No membership fee, no equipment to buy, no uniform. Cost is never the reason a young person in the east of the Island stays at home.

Our Story

Built by the Parish, for the Parish

The Maufant Youth Project grew out of a simple local question: what is there for young people in the east of the Island once the school day ends? From that question, a volunteer committee, a set of keys to the Centre, and a lot of evenings spent listening to teenagers.

Today we are a registered Jersey charity (No. 340, AJC 505) run by local trustees. We work in partnership with families, schools and parishes — and the Centre is also used by the wider community, from polling days to open days.

Get Involved

Four Ways to Keep the Lights On

The Project runs on a rota of local people. Some give an evening a week, some give a van, some give £20 a year. All of it keeps the Centre open.

01 — Volunteer

Give an evening a week

Session helpers make the club possible: setting up the hall, running the tuck counter, refereeing the tournament, being another trusted adult in the room. You do not need a youth work qualification — you need to be reliable, and to like teenagers. References and a safeguarding check are part of the process.

02 — Share a skill

Teach something you already know

Six weeks of guitar, a bread-making evening, a first-aid session, a talk about what your job actually involves. Members vote on what they want next, and we go looking for someone local who can deliver it. That someone is often a parent or a neighbour.

03 — Donate or fundraise

Fund a term, a trip or a van

We hold no core public funding, so snacks, equipment, coach hire and free holiday places all come from Islanders, local businesses and grants. One-off gifts, standing orders, a workplace collection or a sponsored walk all land in the same place: the programme.

04 — Refer a young person

Point someone our way

Teachers, social workers, GPs, parents and neighbours — if you know a young person in the east of the Island who would do better with somewhere to go on a Tuesday night, tell them about us, or get in touch and we will take it from there. Nobody has to be referred to walk in.

Where Your Money Goes

Transparent to the last pound
